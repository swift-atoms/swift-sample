# swift-sample

Use this package when you have repeated measurements (benchmark timings, latencies, counters) and need order statistics, dispersion, a baseline-versus-current verdict, a linear fit or a monotonic-trend test over them. Start from `Sample.Batch<Element>` for a finished set of observations, `Sample.Accumulator` for streaming `UInt64` counters that are merged across workers, `Sample.Comparison<Element>` to judge a change against a baseline, `Sample.Regression.linear(_:)` for a least-squares fit, and `Sample.Trend.mannKendall(_:value:)` for trend evidence over an ordered sequence. Arithmetic on an arbitrary element type goes through a `Sample.Averaging<Element>` witness.

## Laws

- `Sample.Batch<Element>` is ordered once, at construction, by the supplied comparator. "Minimum", "maximum" and every percentile are positions in that order, not numeric extrema: under `.descending`, `min` is the largest value. Nothing else is cached; every statistic is recomputed on each access.
- `percentile(_:)` is nearest-rank by truncation: for p it selects index min(⌊count · p⌋, count − 1). It is `nil` for an empty batch and for any p outside the closed interval 0…1, including NaN and the infinities. p = 0 selects the first element and p = 1 the last.
- `median` and `p50` are `percentile(_:)` at 0.5, so for an even count they are the upper of the two middle elements, never their average.
- `mean` adds every element to the witness's `zero` and divides once by the count, so integer means truncate. It is `nil` for an empty batch.
- `standardDeviation` is the sample standard deviation (denominator count − 1), computed in `Double` through `project` and returned through `embed`. It is `nil` below two observations.
- `coefficientOfVariation` is standard deviation over mean, as a percentage (× 100). It is `nil` below two observations and when the projected mean is zero.
- `medianAbsoluteDeviation` is the median of |x − median|, unscaled (no 1.4826 normal-consistency factor). The inner median averages the two middle deviations for an even count, while the centre is the batch's upper-middle `median`. A single observation has deviation zero.
- `outlierCount(threshold:)` counts observations with |x − median| strictly greater than threshold × MAD, with threshold 3 by default. When the MAD is zero it reports zero outliers, and for an empty batch it reports `nil`.
- `Sample.Averaging<Element>.duration` projects to seconds by converting the whole-second and attosecond parts separately and adding them. This matches `Swift.Duration.components` bit for bit and stays finite over the full `Int128` attosecond range. It deliberately rounds differently from `Swift.Duration.inSeconds`. `embed` goes back through `.seconds(_:)`.
- `Sample.Comparison<Element>.change` is the relative change (current − baseline) / baseline of the chosen `Sample.Metric`, as a fraction, not a percentage. It is `nil` when either metric is absent or the projected baseline is zero. A `nil` change is neither a regression nor an improvement and exceeds no tolerance. A zero change is neither a regression nor an improvement. `exceedsTolerance(_:)` compares the absolute relative change strictly.
- `Sample.Accumulator` forms a commutative monoid under `merged(with:)` with identity `.empty`, whose `minimum` is the largest `UInt64` and whose `maximum` is 0. `monoid` and `commutativeMonoid` are the same witness. `count` and `sum` wrap on overflow. `mean` is truncating integer division and is `nil` when nothing was recorded.
- `Sample.Regression.linear(_:)` is ordinary least squares over (x, y) pairs. It throws `.insufficientPoints` for fewer than two points. It throws `.constantX` when every x value is equal or n·Σx² − (Σx)² is exactly zero; equal x values such as three copies of 0.3 throw even though the sums leave a rounding residue. R² is 0 when y has no variance. The mean squared error divides the residual sum of squares by n − 2 when n > 2, and is the plain residual sum when n = 2.
- `Sample.Trend.mannKendall(_:value:)` takes the sequence order as the time order. Observations whose projected value is not finite are dropped and counted in `excluded`. The variance is tie-corrected, and `ties` counts the distinct values that occur more than once. `standardized` applies the ±1 continuity correction and is 0 when the statistic or the variance is 0. Fewer than two included observations give a statistic, variance and standardized value of 0. No significance decision is made; the caller compares `standardized` against the normal quantile it chooses.
